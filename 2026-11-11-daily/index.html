Morning Briefing

World

Abu Dhabi

The two-day trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi ended with agreement on a large prisoner-of-war exchange. President Zelenskyy called the first day constructive and substantive. No ceasefire text was published, and Russian strikes on Ukraine’s power grid continued overnight.

Gloucester

British counterterrorism police arrested a 41-year-old U.S. Army officer near Gloucester on suspicion of assisting Russian intelligence. He was assigned to NATO’s Allied Rapid Reaction Corps, the alliance’s high-readiness headquarters. He has not been charged, and the Kremlin denied any connection.

Antalya

At COP31 in Antalya, U.N. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres told world leaders the slow response to climate change has put the world on a path to destruction. Turkey is pushing a deal to lift electricity to 35% of global energy use by 2035, and the U.S. is staying away for a second year.

US

Iran Plots

The Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks across the U.S. and Europe, including a plot at a British air base that houses American aircraft. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has reportedly moved to a military base after Iranian threats against his life.

Veterans Day

Wednesday is Veterans Day, a federal holiday. The President took part in the national observance at Arlington National Cemetery, the House held no votes, and the Senate convened at noon to resume work on the permitting bill, H.R. 2347, which it voted 62-21 to take up on Tuesday. Nine Kennedy family members also asked a court to join the lawsuits against the planned 250-foot arch near Arlington, saying it would shadow JFK’s grave.

Markets

CoreWeave’s backlog reaches $132.5 billion

Third-quarter revenue rose 168% to $3.66 billion, ahead of the $3.55 billion analysts expected, and CoreWeave raised its 2026 revenue guidance to $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion. The contracted backlog, a record, is about 36 times the quarter’s revenue. The stock jumped about 13% on Wednesday.

The build is the bill: $37 billion to $39 billion of capital spending planned for 2026, against a quarterly net loss that widened to $709 million.

A CoreWeave data center. The company ended September with about $46.9 billion of debt and 1.7 gigawatts of active power. Photo: ZeroHedge.

Nvidia

Nvidia claws back 3% as shareholders sue

Nvidia recovered about 3% on Wednesday after two losing sessions tied to the Huang spy scandal. Shareholders have now sued the company and its chief executive over that scandal, even as the stock took back part of the fall.

The Tape

Brent climbs 1.2% as Iran deal hopes fade

The rebound ran against the macro tape, not with it. October inflation rose 0.4% against 0.3% expected, core rose 0.3% for a third straight month, and traders put the odds of a December rate hike near 90%. Buyers decided the AI trade mattered more for one session.

Oil reversed its recent slide as hopes of a U.S. and Iran deal faded, with Brent up about 1.2% to just under $93 a barrel. Gold was around $4,050 an ounce, and Bitcoin traded below $74,000.

Worth Knowing

Marvell bids $115 billion for Lumentum

  • $115B

    Marvell’s all-stock price for Lumentum, announced November 2. It is 11.5 times Marvell’s previous biggest deal, the $10 billion purchase of Inphi in 2021. Only Broadcom’s $130 billion bid for Qualcomm, blocked in 2018, was ever larger in tech.

  • $5.8B

    What Marvell owes Lumentum if its board walks away to accept a rival offer. If regulators block the deal instead, the fee is $3.2 billion.

  • $2B × 2

    Nvidia bought $2 billion stakes in both Marvell and Lumentum in March. The merger folds them into one larger stake in the combined company.

  • 2029

    Lumentum’s chief executive says its laser capacity is sold out through early 2029. That shortage is what Marvell is paying to own.

Congo

The Congo’s Ebola outbreak has reached 11,042 confirmed cases and at least 5,387 deaths in government data released Tuesday. The Bundibugyo strain outbreak is now the second largest and second deadliest on record, and it is drawing a fraction of the attention the Iran and Ukraine stories get.
