World

Gaza

Strike on Nasser Hospital kills 6, including a surgeon

An Israeli airstrike hit Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis early Thursday, killing at least 6 people and wounding about 20, according to Gaza's Civil Defence agency and the hospital.

The strike wrecked the surgery department of the largest hospital in southern Gaza. It is the day's sharpest escalation in a war that was supposed to be under a ceasefire.

Ukraine
850,000

people lost power after Russian strikes on the grid

The outages run across the Dnipropetrovsk and Kharkiv regions. In Kyiv, the mayor said a drone strike on an apartment block killed 1 woman and injured 5 others.

Moscow

Envoys meet Putin after Abu Dhabi ends without a ceasefire

Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner arrived in Moscow on Thursday to put the Abu Dhabi results to Vladimir Putin in person.

Two days of US-brokered talks produced a promised prisoner exchange and nothing on stopping the fighting. The strikes on the grid went on through them.

Indonesia

Vice President Gibran submits his resignation to Parliament

Gibran Rakabuming Raka resigned on Thursday, two days after telling President Prabowo Subianto, as Parliament prepared to unveil an impeachment motion against him.

Taiwan Strait

G7 says China's moves east of Taiwan threaten stability

Foreign ministers said in a joint declaration Wednesday night that China's increasingly assertive actions in the waters east of Taiwan threaten regional stability and freedom of navigation.

US

Brooklyn, New York

8 dead, at least 17 injured as Flatbush building falls into a construction pit

The three-story building at 1321 Flatbush Avenue collapsed into the open pit beside it at about 4 p.m. Wednesday. Mayor Zohran Mamdani confirmed 8 dead on Thursday morning, including victims pulled from the rubble and two who died at a hospital.

At least 17 people were injured, among them 7 children and 3 firefighters. The Department of Buildings is investigating the cause, and officials say the search will take days.

Washington

House shelves the Hegseth impeachment articles, 211 to 204

The House voted Tuesday to send Rep. Thomas Massie's articles against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, which ends them for now. Eleven Democrats missed the vote.

Justice Department

Craig Trainor will temporarily run the Civil Rights Division

President Trump named Trainor, a housing official who wrote the administration's 2025 letter to schools on diversity programs, as acting head of the division that enforces federal civil rights law.

Public health

Measles cases pass 5,400, the worst US outbreak since 1991

A regional WHO body opened a four-day meeting on whether the United States has kept its measles elimination status. The reporting so far points to no.

The CDC chief says the response will not change either way. The number is the story: more than 5,400 cases in a country that eliminated the disease a generation ago.

Houston & Texas

Houston · Energy

Citgo posts a record $2.2 billion quarter in Houston

The Houston-headquartered refiner made $2.2 billion in the third quarter, up from $167 million a year earlier, on strong refining margins and record plant performance. EBITDA was $3.0 billion, up from $363 million.

Its three refineries ran 904,000 barrels a day, and it now guides full-year EBITDA of $7.2 billion to $7.5 billion. Who owns it is still unsettled: a court-approved $5.9 billion bid from an Elliott affiliate waits on the US Treasury.

Houston · Astros

Brad Ausmus is the Astros' 21st manager

The club named Ausmus, 57, on Wednesday. He played 10 of his 18 seasons as a catcher in Houston and replaces Joe Espada, whose contract was not renewed after a Wild Card sweep.

He is the first big hire for new general manager Randy Flores and is introduced Friday at Daikin Park.

Houston · City Hall

Freedom Act passed with 71.5%, and Council still has to adopt it

About 413,000 Houston voters backed Proposition A on November 3. It deprioritizes arrests for misdemeanor marijuana possession under 4 ounces and bans no-knock warrants.

Mayor Whitmire says HPD already treats both as low priority and that officers will be trained on the new policy once Council formally adopts the ordinance.

Texas · Austin

Goodwin wants Texas out of its ICE agreements before she takes office

Lt. Gov.-elect Vikki Goodwin said Monday she wants the state to undo its 287(g) agreements with ICE. State law requires sheriffs who run jails to seek those agreements by December 1, 2026, weeks before she is sworn in.

She beat Dan Patrick 51.7% to 45.6% and becomes the first woman in the office and the first Democrat elected to it since 1994.

Markets

Thursday morning

Nvidia fell 10.3% before the open, to $155.91

That was the price at 9:03 a.m. Eastern, against a regular close of $173.73 on Wednesday. The stock was already down more than a fifth over the past month before the resignation.

Kress fronts earnings on Tuesday, November 17, five days into a job she did not apply for in public. The roadmap, the company says, does not change.

Earnings

CoreWeave jumped 24.7% and Apple closed at a record $355.63

CoreWeave's third-quarter revenue was $3.66 billion, and it raised full-year guidance to $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion. That report, out Tuesday night, is what pulled the whole AI trade back up on Wednesday.

Apple rose 2.0% on Wednesday to a fresh record close. Both moves are now being repriced against a Nvidia with no founder at the top.

Worth Knowing

Florida coast

The SS United States is now the world's largest artificial reef

The largest passenger ship built in the United States was deliberately sunk off Florida on Wednesday. Preservationists had fought to make it a museum. It will be a dive site instead.

Autos

World EV sales rose 8.0% in October, to 2.09 million

The rebound puts global electric sales back on a growth line after a soft stretch. One month, one number, and it is the one car people will cite at lunch.

Spain

Spain's first general strike in 14 years was about rent

Wednesday's nationwide strike, the first in 14 years, ran on housing costs and wages. A country with a famous housing squeeze finally used its biggest labor weapon.
