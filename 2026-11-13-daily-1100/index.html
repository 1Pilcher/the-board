Chips Lead a Slide as Wholesale Prices Hit a Four-Year High

Stocks fell this morning as chip shares dropped about 5 percent and October wholesale prices rose 6.5 percent over a year. Europe is set to join the next Ukraine talks, Senate Democrats face a leadership fight, and Houston schools prepare for a handover in January.

By Josh Pilcher

Markets

The markets

Indexes slide at midmorning, with the Nasdaq down 1.4 percent

Stocks gave up early gains this morning. At 10:16 a.m. ET the Nasdaq Composite was down 1.4 percent, the S&P 500 was down 0.9 percent and the Dow was down 0.4 percent, as chip shares led the fall and bond yields stayed above 5 percent.

The week is still positive. The S&P 500 and the Nasdaq came into today up about 3 percent for the week and the Dow up 2 percent, on track for a first winning week in more than a month after four losing weeks.

The economy

Wholesale prices rose 6.5 percent over a year, the most since 2022

The producer price index, what US producers charge before goods reach shoppers, rose 0.3 percent in October, less than the 0.4 percent economists expected and far below the revised 1.4 percent jump in September. Over a year it rose 6.5 percent, the largest annual gain since November 2022.

The Federal Reserve sets short-term interest rates, now at 3.75 to 4.00 percent, and meets on December 8 and 9. Traders put the chance of a quarter-point hike in December at 89 percent, which would lift the range to 4.00 to 4.25 percent. The 10-year Treasury yield, the rate that steers mortgages and business loans, sat at 5.14 percent this morning.

Fed Governor Michael Barr warned yesterday at Yale that the country should prepare for serious short-term disruption to jobs from artificial intelligence. The labor share, the slice of national output paid out as wages, hit a record low of 52.8 percent in the second quarter.

Business

Nvidia falls again after its chief executive resigned yesterday

Jensen Huang resigned yesterday as chief executive of Nvidia, the company that supplies most of the chips used to train artificial intelligence, effective at once. Colette Kress, the finance chief, is interim chief executive while the board searches. The stock closed yesterday at $166.19, down 4.3 percent after falling more than 14 percent during the day, and dropped again this morning. Nvidia reports earnings on Tuesday.

The Justice Department has not concluded whether Alice Huang, the businesswoman linked to Huang, was a Chinese spy, Attorney General Todd Blanche said yesterday. The FBI is investigating.

One chip name rose. Cerebras, a smaller AI chip company, jumped more than 11 percent after raising its 2026 revenue forecast and naming Microsoft as a customer. Cisco, the networking equipment maker, posted strong results and a bright outlook but still fell 4 percent this morning. Apple rose more than 1 percent to a record high.

World

Dublin and Moscow

Europe will join the next Ukraine peace talks, US envoys say

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met President Vladimir Putin in the Kremlin yesterday, then flew to Ireland to brief President Volodymyr Zelensky at an airfield outside Dublin. Kushner said both sides agreed that Europe will sit in the negotiating team at the next round of talks on ending Russia's war in Ukraine, and Zelensky said the next exchange of prisoners will be the biggest of the war.

Moscow has not agreed in public. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said today that Russia would decide once it understood Europe's intentions, and Europe itself has named no representative. More than 40 European leaders met in Dublin yesterday and left the seat unfilled. On the ground, at least 10 people were killed in Ukraine overnight, and Russia said it shot down nearly 1,200 Ukrainian drones.

The Gulf

US downs two Iranian drones as Iran claims a radar hit

The US military said it shot down two Iranian attack drones headed toward Bahrain today, with no injuries and no damage. Bahrain, a Gulf island kingdom, hosts the headquarters of the US Navy's Fifth Fleet.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed it destroyed a US early warning radar there, in answer to a US strike on a telecommunications pole at Sirik in southern Iran. It also said a gas tanker hit a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway at the Gulf's mouth that carries a large share of the world's oil and gas. Neither claim was independently verified, and the British sea monitoring agency UKMTO reported no matching tanker incident.

Gaza

Shelling kills three in Gaza, and a baby dies of cold in a tent

Israeli tank shelling in Deir al-Balah, in central Gaza, killed a woman, a father and his 12-year-old son today and wounded 20 other people, most of them children, health officials at Al-Aqsa Martyrs hospital said.

A boy of 9 months died of hypothermia in a tent flooded by rain this week, the first reported cold death of this winter. Most of Gaza's roughly 2 million people now live in tents or makeshift shelters. Gaza's Health Ministry says at least 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire deal reached more than a year ago.

Jakarta

Indonesia will choose a new vice president on December 2

Gibran Rakabuming Raka resigned this week as Indonesia's vice president to avoid impeachment, after the country's top courts ruled he lacked the education credentials to run for the job. He is the son of former President Joko Widodo.

President Prabowo Subianto picked Gibran as his running mate in the 2024 election, joining two rival camps. Parliament's upper house will pick the replacement on December 2, its speaker said today, and the resignation is widely read as that alliance breaking apart.

US

Washington

Van Hollen challenges Schumer to lead Senate Democrats

Senator Chris Van Hollen of Maryland, 67, said yesterday he will challenge Senator Chuck Schumer of New York, 75, for the job of Senate majority leader. Schumer has led Senate Democrats since 2017. Democrats flipped eight seats in this month's midterm elections and will hold at least 55 of the Senate's 100 seats in January, so their leader will run the chamber.

Van Hollen says voters rejected the status quo and the party needs new leadership. Schumer says he has the votes to win. The caucus votes by closed ballot on December 1, and the winner needs a simple majority. Van Hollen concedes he is the underdog; he counts more than 10 commitments so far.

New York

Brooklyn collapse kills eight; prosecutors open a criminal inquiry

A three-story apartment building on Flatbush Avenue in Brooklyn, built in 1930, collapsed into an excavation next door on Wednesday. Eight people died, two of them children of 9 and 10, and 18 were injured, including four firefighters. The search of the rubble has ended.

Brooklyn District Attorney Eric Gonzalez is examining whether any criminal conduct contributed to the collapse. Mayor Zohran Mamdani says five companies tied to the neighboring site are linked to about 100 other sites across the city, which inspectors began sweeping yesterday. A contractor next door paid a $10,000 fine in April for demolition work carried out under a stop-work order.

Technology

Microsoft outage enters a second day, with about 20 products degraded

A failure in Microsoft's workplace software, which began yesterday, continued this morning. Outlook email, email delivery and the Copilot assistant were still disrupted, and the company's health page listed about 20 products as degraded late this morning. Teams calls and chats were largely working.

The outage tracker Downdetector logged reports from 74 countries. Outlook reports peaked at about 130,000 yesterday and had fallen to about 3,900 by late this morning. Hospitals were among those hit, including Memorial Hermann and Harris Health in Texas. Microsoft says there is no evidence of an attack, and that its capacity has not yet fully returned to normal.

Mississippi

Mississippi elects its first Black senator since 1874

Scott Colom, a Democrat, defeated Cindy Hyde-Smith, the Republican incumbent, in Mississippi's Senate race, in results projected on Wednesday. He becomes the state's first Black senator since the Reconstruction era that followed the Civil War, an upset in a heavily Republican state that widened the Democrats' midterm gains.

Trump threatens to double tariffs on Mexico over migrants

President Donald Trump threatened yesterday to double tariffs on Mexico if it does not stop a group of more than 1,000 migrants walking north toward the US border, warning that they will not be admitted. A tariff is a tax collected on imports at the border, so a doubling would raise the US price of goods shipped from Mexico.

Houston & Texas

Houston

Houston schools plan for elected trustees to be ready by mid-January

Houston ISD, the school district for most of Houston, has been run since June 2023 by a state-appointed Board of Managers. The Texas Education Agency took control after years of failing grades at one high school and turmoil on the elected board, and the appointed managers replaced the elected trustees' authority.

Last night the board's president, Ric Campo, said the managers have begun meeting the elected trustees, are assembling a full briefing package on finances, facilities, transport and staffing, and are planning as if the trustees must be ready by mid-January 2027. Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa, who takes office on January 19, has pledged to replace the state education commissioner and end state takeovers. The managers still voted 7 to 0 last night to approve a $92 million career and technical education center, over the elected trustees' request to wait.

Houston

Houston faces its earliest flu peak since the 2009 swine flu season

Flu cases are climbing early in Houston. Dr. Linda Yancy, an infectious disease specialist at Memorial Hermann, says the case curve is accelerating now, making this the earliest flu peak since the 2009 to 2010 swine flu pandemic.

Texas activity is still rated moderate, but Louisiana next door is already high, and state data show emergency department visits for flu rising. Nationally, more than 9 percent of flu tests are coming back positive. A flu shot takes about three weeks to reach full strength, which puts protection for holiday travel on a clock that starts now.

Texas

Starship tries to catch both of its stages in South Texas today

SpaceX plans to launch Starship, its 407-foot rocket, at 4:45 p.m. CT today from Starbase, the company's site at the southern tip of Texas. It is the rocket's 15th flight since 2023 and it carries 26 working Starlink internet satellites.

The plan is a first. The Super Heavy booster is to fly back and be caught by the launch tower's arms about 10 minutes after liftoff, as has been done before. The upper stage is then to circle Earth 14 times and be caught at the same tower at about 2:45 p.m. CT tomorrow, instead of being dropped in the ocean. As of this morning, SpaceX had not yet received its launch license from the Federal Aviation Administration.

Standard setting

SEC chair backs a FASB research project on data centers and private credit

Paul Atkins, the chairman of the Securities and Exchange Commission, spoke yesterday at a financial reporting conference and praised a new research project at the FASB, the board that writes US accounting rules. The project, called Current Trends and Emerging Issues, tracks data center investment, non-traditional lending and twice-a-year company reporting, and it has already produced a formal project on disclosure for private credit, the market where funds lend directly to companies outside the banking system.

Atkins told both the FASB and the PCAOB, the board that oversees auditors, to keep their agendas on issues that are material to investors and to weigh the cost of any new rule against its benefit.

More: SEC
Audit oversight

SEC approves softer audit quality rules, dropping the outside check

The SEC has approved changes to QC 1000, the PCAOB standard that governs how audit firms run their own quality control, the internal system meant to catch weak audit work before investors rely on it. The changes take effect on December 15.

They drop the requirement that a firm issuing more than 100 audits in a year set up an External QC Function, outside reviewers who grade the firm's assessment of itself. They also drop the rule that a firm build a quality system before it has any public company audit clients. The four largest firms, KPMG, PwC, Deloitte and EY, supported the changes. The CFA Institute, which represents investment analysts, opposed dropping the external check.
