Chip Stocks Slide Again as Nvidia Ends a $650 Billion Week

Stocks fell into the afternoon as chipmakers sold off again. Europe won a seat at the Ukraine peace table, Microsoft traced its global outage to a network upgrade, and a South Texas rocket gets ready to catch itself tonight.

The Economy

Wholesale prices rose 0.3% in October, cooler than forecast

The producer price index, which tracks what businesses charge each other, rose 0.3% last month against a 0.4% forecast, a sharp cooling from a 1.4% jump in September. The annual rate still reached 6.5%, the highest since November 2022, which is why one soft month does not settle the inflation fight.

The Economy

Fed governor warns AI could displace workers at scale

Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr said the country should prepare for serious short term disruption as artificial intelligence spreads through workplaces. Labor took a record low 52.8% of national income in the second quarter, and Barr argued the AI buildout is pushing prices up today, on phones, game consoles and construction wages, whatever it does for productivity later. The Fed holds a public forum on AI and the economy in San Francisco on Monday.

More: Reuters
Business

Microsoft traces its global outage to a network upgrade

The outage that knocked out Outlook, Teams and Copilot for users in 74 countries yesterday entered a second day today before easing. Microsoft said the cause was not a cyberattack and not AI: a flaw introduced during an earlier network capacity upgrade surfaced after a recent network change. Outlook problem reports, which peaked above 130,000 yesterday, were down to almost none by early this afternoon. The company promised a full post incident report.

More: USA Today
Business

Private credit lenders are merging to get bigger

BCP Investment agreed to buy fellow lender Investcorp Credit Management at 90% of its adjusted net asset value, two weeks after Ares Capital struck a $2.6 billion deal for MidCap Financial. These firms, called business development companies, lend to midsize private companies. Small ones are getting squeezed: BCP's quarterly investment income fell to $4.6 million from $5.5 million, and its asset value per share dropped 9.4%.

Dublin and Moscow

Europe gets a seat at the Ukraine peace talks

American envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met President Vladimir Putin in the Kremlin yesterday, then President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Dublin last night. Kyiv and Washington say European representatives will join the negotiating team at the next round, after direct Russian and Ukrainian talks in Abu Dhabi this week, the first in nine months. The two sides also agreed the outline of a prisoner exchange both call the largest of the war.

Moscow has not confirmed the European seat. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said today that Russia is prepared to talk if the Europeans name someone authorized to negotiate, and no European representative has been named. Land and security guarantees, the core of the war, remain unsettled.

The Gulf

US downs 2 Iranian drones as Tehran claims a radar hit

The US military said it shot down two Iranian attack drones headed toward Bahrain today, with no injuries or damage. Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed it destroyed an American early warning radar in Bahrain, which hosts the US Navy's Fifth Fleet, in retaliation for a strike on a communications tower in southern Iran. Neither that claim nor Iran's report of a gas tanker hitting a mine in the Strait of Hormuz, the waterway that carries a large share of the world's oil, could be independently verified.

Gaza

Rain floods 46,000 Gaza households as 150,000 tents wait outside

Heavy rain since November 6 has flooded displacement camps across Gaza, where about 1.4 million people, roughly two thirds of the population, live in tents or makeshift shelters. The UN humanitarian office counts about 46,000 households affected and more than 40,000 shelters damaged. Aid agencies appealed today for winter supplies to be let in: more than 150,000 tents sit outside Gaza, ready to ship, awaiting Israeli permission at the checkpoints.

Delhi

Delhi overtakes Lahore as the world's most polluted city

India's capital passed Lahore in Pakistan at the top of the global pollution ranking today, as smog cut visibility across a city of 20 million people. The Swiss monitor IQAir put Delhi's air quality index at 334, deep in hazardous territory, where a reading of 50 or below counts as good. India's own pollution board measured 403 and rated the air severe.

Mississippi

Mississippi elects its first Black senator since 1881

Scott Colom, a 43 year old district attorney, defeated Republican Senator Cindy Hyde Smith with 49.2% of the vote to her 48.7%. He is the first Black senator from Mississippi since Blanche K. Bruce, who was born into slavery and left the Senate in 1881, and the first Democrat the state has sent there since 1982. Mississippi has the largest Black share of any state's population, at almost 40%.

New York

The Brooklyn collapse is now a criminal case

A three story apartment building from 1930 fell into a construction excavation next door in the Flatbush neighborhood on Wednesday afternoon. Firefighters ended their search yesterday and found no more victims. Brooklyn's district attorney has opened a criminal investigation, and the mayor ordered a review of about 100 sites tied to five entities connected to the construction project. The Buildings Department briefly posted, then withdrew as premature, a notice blaming unsafe work at the neighboring site. The cause is still under investigation.

Houston & Texas

Katy

Four arrested as the Houston poker crackdown widens

Officers raided Katy Poker, an eight table cardroom in Katy, yesterday and arrested four people on charges of organized criminal activity, with money laundering charges possible. Harris County District Attorney Sean Teare says the room took a cut, called a rake, straight out of cash game pots. Texas law allows poker clubs that charge membership and seat fees instead of a rake, and Teare says those clubs are not targets.

It is the second Katy room raided in recent weeks. Another, 101 Richmond, reopened within a day of a raid last month in which officers seized $118,000 in cash. No players were arrested in either raid.

Starbase, South Texas

SpaceX tries to catch both halves of its biggest rocket tonight

Starship, the largest rocket ever flown, is set to lift off from Starbase at the southern tip of Texas in a 75 minute window opening at 4:45 PM CT today. The plan is a first: catch the Super Heavy booster at the launch tower about seven minutes after liftoff, send the upper stage around Earth about 14 times, and catch it at the same tower when it returns tomorrow afternoon. As of early this afternoon, the Federal Aviation Administration had not yet issued the launch license.

Washington

Audit regulator sanctions 5 firms and 7 people

The Public Company Accounting Oversight Board, the body that polices auditors of public companies, announced settled orders today covering audit failures, violations of its engagement quality review rules, and refusal to cooperate with an investigation. Four firms and six of the individuals admitted to the findings. Some firms let audit reports go out without the required second partner review. One firm never told regulators that a client had filed incorrect financial statements, which the securities laws require. The cases grew out of the board's inspection program.

More: PCAOB
Washington

Plan to drop control audits for about 81% of companies draws fire

A Securities and Exchange Commission proposal would exempt about 81% of public companies from the Sarbanes Oxley rule that requires an outside audit of internal controls, the safeguards meant to catch cooked books. The Center for Audit Quality and the American Accounting Association both pushed back in comment letters. SEC leaders defended the plan at an industry conference this week as cutting costs that land on shareholders.

More: Accounting Today
Washington

Audit watchdog splits enforcement into 5 specialist units

The PCAOB has reorganized the division that investigates auditors into five units: strategic risk, super intelligence, global network firms, smaller unaffiliated firms, and international cases. The split puts dedicated teams on fraud warning signs, on risks tied to artificial intelligence, and on the giant firm networks that audit most big public companies.
