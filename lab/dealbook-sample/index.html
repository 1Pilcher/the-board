FAA Halts Every Departure in the Country After a Telecom Outage

One network failure stopped American aviation. The market’s biggest company finished its first week without its founder. Wholesale inflation printed its worst annual number in four years. And everywhere else the day turned, it turned on a single link.

By Josh Pilcher
Scroll

Verdict

Friday kept finding the single link. In the afternoon, one telecommunications network failed and every departing flight in the United States stopped. The largest company in the world closed its first full week without its founder, run by its finance chief, its stock down about an eighth in five days. Wholesale inflation printed its highest annual reading since 2022, and that one number now hangs over the Federal Reserve’s December meeting. The Ukraine talks produced a chair with nobody named to sit in it. The only rocket that flew all day missed the maneuver the day was built around. Nothing failed in general. Each thing failed at exactly one point, and the point was the story.

The Lead

One Network Failed, and Every Departure in the Country Stopped

The order came at 3:54 p.m. Eastern, six minutes before the stock market closed: every departing flight in the United States was to stay on the ground until further notice. The Federal Aviation Administration cited extraordinary circumstances. The cause, it said, was a telecommunications outage at the centers that guide aircraft between airports, all 22 of them at once.

Planes already in the air, about 3,100 of them bound for American airports, kept flying and landed. Everything on the ground stayed there. By 5:40 p.m., nearly 10,000 flights had been delayed and more than 1,100 canceled, on FlightAware’s count. It was the second nationwide halt to departures since September 11, 2001. The last one, in January 2023, was also a single system failing: the pilot-alert database.

The en route centers are the layer of the system nobody sees. Towers handle takeoffs and landings. The centers hand aircraft across the sky in between, and they run on one telecommunications network. People briefed on the failure said the outage sat in that network, which is overseen by L3Harris Technologies. When it went, no second path was carrying the load at national scale, so the safe answer was the total one: stop everything that had not yet left.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no indication of an attack and no timeline for a fix. The cause has not been confirmed. Late in the afternoon the FAA began releasing departures in coordination with its command center as the centers recovered, with the next update due at 11 p.m. Eastern.

One departure did get cleared. At 5:47 p.m. Eastern, while the stop was still in force, SpaceX launched its largest rocket from the Texas coast, under its own license, on a flight the ground stop never governed. The contrast is the day in one frame: the routine system, layered over decades, stopped cold by one network, and the experimental one flew. What the stop costs arrives over the weekend, as aircraft and crews sit in the wrong cities and airlines rebuild their schedules around them. Airline shares had six minutes of trading after the order. The bill gets priced on Monday.

The Board Daily

Markets

The market’s leadership is one company, and its December decision is one number.

Nasdaq Falls 0.9% as Nvidia Ends Its First Week Without Huang Down About 13%

The Dow rose. Everything else fell, and the Nasdaq fell most, because the selling lived where it has lived all week: in the chip stocks that carry the market’s leadership.

Nvidia fell 2.0% on the day to $162.85, which makes about 13% lost in the week since Jensen Huang’s resignation. The company is run tonight by its finance chief, Colette Kress, as interim chief executive, and on Tuesday she presents the quarter he was supposed to present. AMD fell 8.8%, the worst drop in the S&P 500, and Cisco fell 6.2%. The exception proves the concentration: Cerebras rose 11.2% after raising its 2026 guidance and naming Microsoft a customer, a challenger gaining on the exact day the incumbent wobbled. The 10-year Treasury yield ended at 5.15%, and the VIX at 19.3.

Producer Prices Rise 6.5% Over the Year, the Fastest Since November 2022

The monthly number was calm and the annual number was not, and the annual number is the one that matters. Producer prices rose 0.3% in October, less than the 0.4% expected. Over twelve months they rose 6.5%, the fastest pace since November 2022.

Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee warned on Friday that inflation is spreading, and named the cost of building AI data centers among the pressures pushing it. Futures pricing now treats a quarter-point rate hike at the Fed’s meeting on December 8 and 9 as close to certain. December’s decision hangs on this print, and on November’s, which arrives the day after the Fed votes.

World

A chair with no name on it, and a strait with one way through.

Europe Wins a Seat at the Ukraine Talks, and Nobody Is Named to Fill It

Thursday’s shuttle diplomacy, two hours with Putin in the Kremlin and one hour with Zelensky at an airfield outside Dublin, produced the week’s agreement: Europe joins the next round of talks inside the negotiating team, and Russia and Ukraine will carry out the largest prisoner exchange of the war.

Both halves have a blank in them. Moscow’s account names no agreement on Europe. The exchange has no settled number: Zelensky has said 1,000 to 1,200 prisoners a side, and Putin’s aide Yuri Ushakov says the parameters are still being agreed. And the seat itself is unclaimed: about 40 European leaders met in Dublin on Thursday and left without choosing who sits in it. A framework with its two load-bearing facts missing is not a settlement. It is a schedule waiting for its facts.

Iran Says It Destroyed a US Radar in Bahrain; the US Says It Downed Two Iranian Drones

The Strait of Hormuz ran its two prices again. Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said on Friday that it had destroyed an American early-warning radar in Bahrain, in retaliation for what it called an American strike on a telecommunications tower at Sirik, on the Iranian coast. The US military said it had intercepted two Iranian attack drones headed toward Bahrain, and denied the radar claim. Neither account is independently verified.

On the water, the Guard said a gas tanker struck a mine in the strait on Friday, two days after two supertankers were disabled in the same waters; Britain’s maritime agency confirmed one vessel hit by a projectile, its engine room on fire. Monitors counted 37 attacks on shipping in October and at least 10 more in the first ten days of November. West Texas crude settled at $89.01, up 0.3%. The benchmark is pricing barrels. The strait keeps pricing the route.

US

Both of the day’s American counts turned on how the counting is done.

Holscher Wins the Kansas Governor’s Race by 7,200 Votes

The last open governor’s race in the country is closed. The Associated Press called Kansas for Democrat Cindy Holscher on Thursday, nine days after election day, at 50.3% to 49.7% over Republican Senate President Ty Masterson, a margin of 7,200 votes on the current count. It is the closest Kansas governor’s race since 1974, and it breaks a pattern held since 1965: Kansas has alternated parties for governor for 60 years, and has now elected two Democrats in a row. The call waited on a state-required audit, triggered because the margin was under one point.

Masterson, who carried President Trump’s endorsement and was rated the Republicans’ best chance to flip any of the year’s 36 governor’s races, conceded the same day. Certification comes December 1. Holscher takes office on January 11.

ICE Reports a Record 483,200 Deportations in Fiscal 2026

The Department of Homeland Security announced on Wednesday that ICE deported 483,200 people in the fiscal year that ended September 30, the most in the agency’s history, alongside more than 491,000 arrests.

The record depends on the counting. Case-level records obtained by Syracuse University’s TRAC show formal removals fell during the year while voluntary departures ordered by immigration judges rose more than tenfold, and the department folds those departures into its total. Both things are true at once: the number is a record on the department’s terms, and it is built differently from the records it is compared against. Which count governs is now a political question with a chart attached.

Houston & Texas

One maneuver missed over the Gulf, and one signature in Houston that binds the next board.

SpaceX Aborts the Booster Catch; the Ship Waits in Orbit for Its Own Attempt Tomorrow

Flight 15 lifted off from Starbase at 4:47 p.m., into a sky the FAA had just emptied. The plan was the day’s most ambitious piece of choreography: catch the Super Heavy booster at the tower about six minutes after liftoff, then catch the ship itself on Saturday, after 14 orbits. The first half failed inside seven minutes. SpaceX aborted the catch for an unknown issue, and Booster 22, the first of the new V3 boosters, went into the Gulf. It was the first catch attempt since Flight 8 in March 2025.

The ship, carrying 26 Starlink satellites, reached orbit and is flying its 22 hours. So the double catch is now a single one, tomorrow afternoon, and it is the half that has never been done: no Starship upper stage has ever been caught.

HISD’s Appointed Board Approves a $92.4 Million Career Center Over Its Successors’ Objections

Houston’s school board spent Thursday night describing its own exit, then signing like it was staying. Board President Ric Campo told the room the district is planning as if the elected trustees must be ready by mid-January 2027, and staff are assembling the transition package for trustees who have sat without a vote since the 2023 state takeover.

Later in the same meeting, the appointed board voted 7-0, with one abstention, to commit $92.4 million to a construction contract for a career and innovation center in Sunnyside. Every elected trustee who spoke asked for a delay. The board declined. Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has pledged to end the takeover, and the departing board’s answer is that the decision could not wait. Its successors inherit the building either way.

The Ledger

The record everyone relied on, withdrawn for the second time in a year.

Driven Brands Withdraws Three Years of Accounts, and Its Auditor’s Report With Them

Driven Brands told the SEC on November 3 that its financial statements for 2023, 2024 and 2025, and for the first two quarters of 2026, should no longer be relied on, and that PricewaterhouseCoopers’ audit report on the 2025 statements should not be relied on either. The errors sit in fixed assets: write-offs missed around sale-leaseback deals, assets misclassified, useful lives set wrong, all of it found while auditing subsidiaries inside its whole-business securitization.

It is the second withdrawal this year; a February conclusion forced a restatement inside the annual report filed in May. The third-quarter report that was due November 5 has not appeared and has no timeline. Buybacks are halted until the company is current, the 2026 outlook is cut, and the cleanup will cost at least $50 million this year. Every valuation of the company runs on those three years. At the moment there is nothing underneath them.

Worth Knowing

The day’s last single assumption, and a free entrée.

A 1960 Paper in Science Said the Population Would Reach Infinity Today

The date in the title arrived: “Doomsday: Friday, 13 November, A.D. 2026.” Three researchers at the University of Illinois fitted one curve to 24 estimates of world population across two thousand years, and the curve, extended, reaches infinity today. It was a good curve. It tracked the real count within about 7% for three decades. Then the world’s growth rate, which had peaked at 2.3% in 1963, kept falling instead of rising, and the model and the world parted company.

Run for last year, the formula returns about 76 billion people. The United Nations counts 8.2 billion. The authors knew the assumption was both the joke and the warning: November 13 is von Foerster’s birthday.

Chipotle Gave Away Entrées for One Hour Today to Anyone With a Tattoo, Drawn-On Included

From 3 to 4 p.m. local time, Chipotle ran its “Tatted Like a Chipotle Bag” offer: show a tattoo, permanent, temporary or drawn on with a marker, and a second entrée was free. The last time the chain ran it, on the previous Friday the 13th, in March, it reported the highest single-day sales in its history.
