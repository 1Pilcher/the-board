The Gospel of Mark

The earliest Gospel, read slowly and honestly: who wrote it, when, what in it can be checked, and why it still sets the terms for everything written after.

This study reads the Bible the slow way: one book at a time, with the history left in and the hard questions left open. It begins with Mark, the earliest Gospel we have, and the shortest.

Mark was written within living memory of the crucifixion, probably around the year 70. It is urgent, plain, and strange, and it ends, in its earliest form, at an empty tomb, with no risen Jesus on stage at all.

THE STUDY

Why The Study exists.

The Study is a slow read through the Bible, one book at a time. It starts with the New Testament, because that's where the biggest question is, then goes back to the Old Testament, which is far older, and reads it on its own terms.

It's not a devotional, and it's not trying to convert anyone, me included. I'm a skeptical reader. I'd like to be a Christian, but I can't get past the case for the resurrection, and picking a religion feels too important to do without proper skepticism and logic. So the questions stay open as long as they need to.

Each session asks the same things. What does the text actually say. Who wrote it, when, and what was going on around them. What's actually historical, graded honestly. What it meant to the people hearing it first. What pattern is running underneath the story. And what its values ended up building in the world we still live in.
