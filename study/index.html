The Gospel of Mark

The earliest Gospel, read slowly and honestly: who wrote it, when, what in it can be checked, and why it still sets the terms for everything written after.

What does the text actually say? Who wrote it, and when? What in it can be checked against the evidence?

What did it mean to the people who heard it first? What pattern runs underneath the story? And what did its values go on to build?

This session takes up the Gospel of Mark, the earliest of the four and the shortest. It was written about forty years after the crucifixion, most likely near the year 70, by an author who never names himself and never claims to have seen the events he describes.

Mark is also where this study has to begin, because of how the book ends, and because its ending is not the one most readers think they know. The case for the resurrection starts here, and it starts in silence.

THE STUDY

Why The Study exists.

I don’t have a ton of time. It’s hard to keep up.

There are so many conflicting claims about the Bible, fed by conflicting interests. It feels hard to know what is true, and I know I’m not alone in that.

I often have questions I want answered, or things I don’t understand, that nobody seems to answer straight. Not all of the time, but sometimes. When that happens, there is nowhere obvious to go for a straight answer.

In my ideal world, I build something useful to me. Something I enjoy reading. Because if I do, so will other people, and it will help them too.

The Study is a personal project. Everything here reflects my own views and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or representative of my employer.
