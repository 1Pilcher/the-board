The Texts Behind Mark

The older scriptures Mark’s first readers already knew by heart: Daniel’s Son of Man, Isaiah’s suffering servant, the psalm that scripts the cross, and Abraham’s bound son.

Session 1 read Mark, the earliest Gospel, on its own terms. This session reads what Mark was read with. Nine older texts, written across roughly six hundred years, supplied the words his Gospel is built from: the voice in the wilderness, the beloved son, the Son of Man, the suffering servant, the psalm that scripts the cross.

The question of the session is simple to state. Before Mark wrote a word, what did his first readers already believe? What was in place, in the texts they had heard all their lives, that made the resurrection claim sayable, and thinkable, in the form it took?

The answer, graded honestly, is that the pieces were all old. A God who raises the dead, a figure called one like a son of man coming with the clouds, a servant who suffers and is vindicated, a bound son given back: every one of these stands in a text that predates Christianity, and the Dead Sea Scrolls prove the texts were being copied and read in the very decades before Mark wrote.

What no surviving pre-Christian text does is put the pieces together into one story about one man. The ingredients are demonstrably pre-Christian. The recipe, as far as the evidence reaches, is not attested before the Christians themselves. The resurrection was not argued from nothing. It was argued from these.

THE STUDY

Why The Study exists.

I don’t have a ton of time. It’s hard to keep up.

There are so many conflicting claims about the Bible, fed by conflicting interests. It feels hard to know what is true, and I know I’m not alone in that.

I often have questions I want answered, or things I don’t understand, that nobody seems to answer straight. Not all of the time, but sometimes. When that happens, there is nowhere obvious to go for a straight answer.

In my ideal world, I build something useful to me. Something I enjoy reading. Because if I do, so will other people, and it will help them too.

The Study is a personal project. Everything here reflects my own views and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or representative of my employer.
