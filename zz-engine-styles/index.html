The Board · Engine Styles

Real engines, famous templates

Same four charts, same numbers, eight ways. Every chart below came out of Matplotlib or Plotly running an established template, selected and left alone: no hand-drawing, no hand-tuning. The templates are on trial exactly as they ship.

Template 01 · Matplotlib · built-in style

FiveThirtyEight

The data-journalism look Matplotlib ships in the box, modeled on FiveThirtyEight's own charts: soft gray ground, bold strokes, nothing on the top or right edge.

Template 02 · Matplotlib · built-in style

ggplot

The house look of R's ggplot2, ported into Matplotlib: gray panel, white gridlines. A statistician's default, familiar everywhere, a little busy.

Template 03 · Matplotlib · built-in style

BMH

From the book Bayesian Methods for Hackers: pale grid, muted colors, figures built to sit quietly inside long technical writing.

Template 04 · Matplotlib via Seaborn

Seaborn · ticks

The statistical library's most restrained theme: white ground, outward ticks only, no grid at all. Seaborn's idea of clean.

Template 05 · Matplotlib via SciencePlots

Science + Nature

SciencePlots' journal styles, set to Nature's rules: drawn at printed-column size for scientific papers. Dense and small by design; paper first, screens second.

Template 06 · Matplotlib · their system in code

The Economist, replicated

The Economist's published chart system rebuilt as code: off-white ground, horizontal grid only, one red that means the point. Not a shipped template; the desk's rules, re-created.

Template 07 · Plotly · built-in template

simple_white

Plotly's own minimal template: white ground, light gray axis lines, the big interactive library's idea of restraint.

Template 08 · Plotly · built-in template

ggplot2

Plotly's port of the ggplot2 look: the same gray panel and white grid, from the other engine.

Sources: Matplotlib's built-in style sheets (matplotlib.org style gallery); Seaborn's theme system (seaborn.pydata.org); SciencePlots journal styles (garrettj403/SciencePlots); Plotly's built-in templates (plotly.com); The Economist's chart style as documented in design-system breakdowns of their published charts. Sample data, same set used across this page: quarterly index returns, a year of monthly closes, household asset shares, Houston and Dallas median home prices.
