Seventeen ways to draw the same numbers

The same four charts, drawn seventeen ways: the quarter's index returns, a year of the index, where household money sits, and home prices in two cities, then against now. Nothing changes between families but the hand doing the drawing. Pick the family, and every chart on the site gets drawn in it.

Style 1 · Blueprint

Blueprint

Drafting-table charts: fine ink linework, hatched fills instead of flat color, dimension marks, and red saved for the one series that matters.

Style 2 · Engraving

Engraving

Etched monochrome, crosshatch on crosshatch, the feel of a graphic cut for an old financial print. The red appears once per chart and means it.

Style 3 · The Ledger

The Ledger

An accountant's worksheet: ruled rows, figures kept in aligned columns, and the double underline where a total lands. Quiet, exact, and personal to the man whose name is on the page.

Style 4 · Swiss Clean

Swiss Clean

Nothing on the page that is not the data: flat ink, one red, hairline baselines, and room to breathe. The labels do the work.

Style 5 · Shape Language

Shape Language

The site's own geometry doing the counting: dots stand in for bars, a field of a hundred dots makes the whole, and rings with cores carry then against now.

Style 6 · Monument

Monument

Thick, solid, and lit from above: every bar and ribbon carries one fall of light, bright at the top edge and deep at the base, the way sun lands on something carved to last.

Style 7 · Cloisonné

Cloisonné

Enamel work: flat, jewel-muted color held inside a clean ink outline, every shape closed by its line.

Style 8 · Ink & Wash

Ink & Wash

Bold ink borders first; inside them, color reduced to a pale wash, so the outline does all the drawing.

Style 9 · Lit & Lined

Lit & Lined

Monument's light, the gradient falling from a lit top edge to a deep base, now held on every side by a full ink border.

Style 10 · Double Rule

Double Rule

Certificate formality: every shape carries two borders, a strong outer rule and a fine inner rule running parallel inside it.

Style 11 · Leadwork

Leadwork

Stained-glass logic in Board colors: thick ink leading between flat, muted fields, every pane held on all sides.

Style 12 · Blueprint, Closed

Blueprint, Closed

The drafting table hardened: hatch fills and dimension marks, with every shape closed in a full ink border and ticks at the corners.

Style 13 · After the Financial Times

After the Financial Times

Stolen from the Financial Times: the salmon-pink panel, horizontal gridlines only, labels inside the chart instead of a legend, and claret kept for the one series that matters.

Style 14 · After The Economist

After The Economist

Stolen from The Economist: the red rule and tag across the top, a white plot on their pale blue-gray ground, horizontal gridlines only, everything left-aligned and a little austere.

Style 15 · After Bloomberg Graphics

After Bloomberg Graphics

Stolen from Bloomberg Graphics: black as the lead series color, the heaviest numerals on the page, an electric violet for the leader and magenta for the loser, and axes that get out of the way.

Style 16 · After the New York Times

After the New York Times

Stolen from the New York Times graphics desk: soft grays on white, no legends anywhere, and the story told in small notes written on the chart itself, at the peak and at the turn.

Style 17 · By the Rules

By the Rules

The Economist's system, applied literally: their eight rules as the build spec. Red reserved for the one series carrying each chart's message, everything else in grays and light reds; headline titles with academic subtitles at half the size; direct labels, no legends, no extra hues, no decoration.
